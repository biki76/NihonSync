Today, I'm sharing the exact study plan I wish I had followed. This isn't theoretical — it's based on my own near-failure, feedback from 2,000+ NihonSync users, and analysis of every N5 exam paper from 2015-2025. Follow this plan, and you'll pass with confidence, not by a hair.

Understanding the JLPT N5 Exam Format

Before we build the plan, let's understand what you're fighting. The JLPT N5 has three sections:

SectionContentTimeScorePassing Threshold
Language Knowledge (Vocabulary)Kanji reading, word meaning, context-appropriate words25 min~60 pointsOverall 80/180
Language Knowledge (Grammar) + ReadingSentence patterns, particle usage, short reading comprehension50 min~60 pointsEach section 19/60 min
ListeningTask-based comprehension, key phrase identification, verbal information30 min~60 pointsNo section can be zero

The total is 180 points. You need 80 to pass. But here's the catch: you can't score zero in any section. I've seen students get 100 total but fail because they got 0 in listening. Balance is mandatory.

The 3-Month Study Plan

This plan assumes you can study 1.5-2 hours per day. If you can only do 1 hour, extend it to 4 months. If you can do 3 hours, you might finish in 2 months. The key is consistency, not intensity.

🟢 Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-4) — Building the Base

Goal: Master Hiragana, Katakana, and 50 core vocabulary words. Learn basic grammar particles.

WeekDaily FocusTimeResources
Week 1Hiragana mastery (あ-の)1.5 hrsNihonSync Hiragana module + paper tracing
Week 2Hiragana completion (は-ん) + 10 vocab1.5 hrsNihonSync + handmade flashcards
Week 3Katakana (ア-ノ) + 20 vocab1.5 hrsNihonSync Katakana + vocabulary list
Week 4Katakana completion (ハ-ン) + particles は・が・を2 hrsNihonSync Lessons 1-3 + grammar notes

Phase 1 Tips:

• Don't skip Katakana. Many N5 words are written in Katakana (パン, レストラン, テレビ).
• Write by hand every day. Digital practice alone isn't enough.
• Record yourself reading Hiragana aloud. Listen back. You'll catch pronunciation errors.
• Study particles with example sentences, not in isolation. は marks topics. が marks subjects. を marks direct objects. に marks direction/time. で marks location of action.

🟡 Phase 2: Expansion (Weeks 5-8) — Grammar & Vocabulary Surge

Goal: Learn 50 more vocabulary words (total 100). Master core N5 grammar. Start reading practice.

Week 5Adjectives (い-adj, な-adj) + 10 vocab2 hrsNihonSync Lessons 4-5 + adjective drills
Week 6Verb groups + conjugation + 10 vocab2 hrsNihonSync Lessons 6-7 + verb tables
Week 7Question forms, past tense, て-form + 10 vocab2 hrsNihonSync Lessons 8-10 + sentence writing
Week 8Reading comprehension practice + 20 vocab2 hrsNihonSync reading passages + past papers

Phase 2 Tips:

• Grammar is best learned through pattern recognition. Don't memorize rules — memorize sentence patterns. 「〜は〜です」→「〜は〜でした」→「〜は〜じゃありません」. See the pattern?
• Write 5 original sentences every day using the grammar you learned. Even simple ones like 「わたしはがくせいです」count.
• Start a "mistake notebook." Every time you get a quiz question wrong, write the correct answer and WHY it's correct. I still have my 2019 mistake notebook — 47 pages of errors that became my greatest teacher.
• Read NHK World Japan's "Easy Japanese" news. It's designed for N5-N4 learners.

🔴 Phase 3: Mastery (Weeks 9-12) — Kanji, Mock Tests & Listening

Goal: Learn 50+ N5 Kanji. Take 5+ mock tests. Master listening comprehension.

WeekDaily FocusTimeResources
Week 9Kanji batch 1 (numbers, nature, people) + listening2 hrsNihonSync Kanji module + NHK audio
Week 10Kanji batch 2 (actions, places, time) + listening2 hrsNihonSync + JLPT listening practice
Week 11Mock test 1-2 + error analysis + weak area focus2.5 hrsOfficial JLPT past papers + NihonSync quizzes
Week 12Mock test 3-5 + final review + exam strategy2.5 hrsTimed practice + relaxation techniques

Phase 3 Tips:

• Kanji seems scary but N5 only requires about 100 characters. Many are simple: 一 (one), 二 (two), 三 (three), 人 (person), 日 (day). Start with the easy ones to build confidence.
• For listening: practice EVERY DAY in Phase 3. Even 15 minutes helps. Listen to Japanese podcasts, anime without subtitles, or NHK news. The key is exposure to natural speed.
• Mock tests should be TIMED. Use a stopwatch. Sit at a desk. No phone. Recreate exam conditions. I used to take mock tests at the same time as the real exam (Sunday morning) to condition my brain.
• In the final week, stop learning new material. Review only. Your brain needs consolidation time.

Listening: The Section That Kills Most Bengali Students

Here's a hard truth: most Bengali students fail or barely pass the listening section. Why?

1. We study reading/writing first. Listening becomes an afterthought.
2. We practice with slow audio. Real JLPT audio is natural speed.
3. We don't practice note-taking. JLPT listening requires quick mental notes.
4. We panic. One missed question leads to missing the next three.

My solution: Start listening practice in Week 1, not Week 9. Even if you only know 10 words, listen to Japanese audio. Your brain needs time to adapt to the sound patterns, pitch accent, and speed.

Listening Practice Method:

• Week 1-4: Listen to Japanese children's songs. Slow, clear, repetitive.
• Week 5-8: NHK Easy Japanese audio + anime with Japanese subtitles.
• Week 9-12: JLPT listening past papers ONLY. No subtitles. One playthrough.

During the actual exam, if you miss a question, FORGET IT immediately. Focus on the next one. Dwelling costs you 2-3 questions. I learned this the hard way.

Exam Day Strategy

The night before: Sleep 8 hours. Don't cram. Your brain needs rest.

Morning of: Eat a light breakfast. Heavy food makes you sleepy. I ate paratha once before a mock test and nearly fell asleep during reading comprehension.

During the exam:

• Vocabulary section: If you don't know a word in 20 seconds, guess and move on. Don't waste time.
• Grammar/Reading: Read the question BEFORE the passage. Know what you're looking for.
• Listening: Look at the answer choices during the pre-question silence. Predict what you might hear.
• Time management: Leave 5 minutes for review. Never leave blanks — guess if you must.

What If You Fail?

I almost failed. Many students do fail on their first attempt. It's not the end of your Japanese journey — it's data.

If you fail, analyze your score report. Which section was weakest? Was it vocabulary? Grammar? Listening? Target that section for 6 weeks and retake. The JLPT is held twice a year (July and December). You have time.

One of my NihonSync users, Rahim from Sylhet, failed N5 twice. On his third attempt, he scored 142/180. He told me, "Biki bhai, failing taught me more than passing would have." He's now preparing for N4.
